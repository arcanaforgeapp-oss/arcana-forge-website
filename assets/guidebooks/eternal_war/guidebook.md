# The Eternal War
## A tarot and zodiac guidebook for Arcana Forge

**90 face cards + 1 shared back | Digital edition**

### The world of this deck

The Eternal War takes place where celestial cities and infernal strongholds meet. Angels offer light, courage, mercy, and protection. Demons embody temptation, deception, domination, and the seductive promise that an easy victory can justify any cost. Between them stand people whose choices give the conflict its meaning.

The central question is not simply who wins a battle. It is what the struggle asks you to become. The deck examines the point where conviction turns into fanaticism, protection into possession, justice into revenge, or generosity into control. It also makes room for repair: a difficult past does not make a better next choice impossible.

Read the imagery as a symbolic story about behavior and responsibility. A person is not an angel or a demon because one card appears beside their name. Examine actions, circumstances, and evidence. The cards are most useful when they help you ask a clearer question and choose a concrete response.

The traditional tarot structure remains intact: 22 Major Arcana and 56 Minor Arcana. Twelve zodiac cards extend the deck's language. The shared back completes the 91 artwork files.

### How the deck is organized

**Major Arcana, 00-21:** Turning points, conscience, identity, power, reckoning, and renewal. These cards describe the larger pattern around a situation.

**Wands, 22-35:** Fire, motivation, courage, ambition, conflict, and leadership. Their battlefield is the direction of your energy.

**Cups, 36-49:** Water, feeling, relationship, compassion, grief, and belonging. Their battlefield is the difference between love and possession.

**Swords, 50-63:** Air, thought, truth, boundaries, argument, and decision. Their battlefield is the use of reason and language.

**Pentacles, 64-77:** Earth, resources, work, care, security, and legacy. Their battlefield is the practical effect of choices on everyday life.

**Zodiac, 78-89:** Twelve temperaments and tensions that can appear in anyone's life. Use them as optional context cards. They do not label a person's character by birth date.

### The visual language

**White feathers and heavenly gold:** Care, clarity, courage, guidance, and the possibility of a principled action.

**Obsidian horns and crimson embers:** Coercion, deception, destructive appetite, and the hidden cost of a tempting bargain.

**Human figures:** Agency. Guidance and pressure may surround a person, but the reading asks what choices remain theirs.

**Swords:** A truth, a boundary, or an idea with consequences. Their sharpness reminds you that even an accurate statement can be used cruelly.

**Cups:** What is felt, offered, received, or withheld. Look for reciprocity and the difference between a gift and a demand.

**Wands:** Living energy and directed intention. Fire can warm a sanctuary or consume it.

**Pentacles:** Value made material. Look beyond possession to the labor, trust, and responsibility sustaining a resource.

**Bridges, gates, boats, and paths:** A transition and the practical conditions that make it possible.

**Ruins and dawn:** The end of an arrangement and the beginnings of recovery. Dawn does not erase what happened; it makes a next step visible.

**Mirrors and eclipses:** An incomplete picture. Check what you are projecting, what is concealed, and what the evidence can support.

### Preparing a reading

1. Choose whether to use the 78 tarot faces alone or add the 12 zodiac faces. Keep the shared back out of the draw.
2. Settle on a question about your own understanding or action: "How can I respond fairly?" or "What is the cost of this choice?"
3. Decide whether to read reversals before shuffling. Both approaches work; consistency helps you interpret the result.
4. Shuffle and draw the cards for your chosen spread. Record the question and card positions.
5. Notice the image before consulting the text. Which figure, object, or relationship first holds your attention?
6. Read each entry in relation to its position. Then look at the connections between cards.
7. End by naming one practical action, one boundary, or one fact to verify.

A useful question leaves room for agency. Instead of "Is this person evil?", ask "Which behavior concerns me, what evidence supports that concern, and how can I respond?" Instead of "Will I win?", ask "What would a worthwhile outcome require, and which methods could undermine it?"

### Upright and shadow or reversed meanings

An upright card presents a quality that may be available, needed, or already active. It is not automatically a promise of a pleasant outcome. The Devil upright, for example, asks for an honest account of attachment and coercion.

A reversed card can show a blocked quality, an excess, an inward process, or a pattern beginning to loosen. It does not automatically mean evil or failure. A reversed Sun may describe difficulty receiving joy; a reversed Devil may describe a bond losing its hold.

If you do not use reversals, read the shadow paragraph whenever the image or surrounding cards suggest imbalance. The light and shadow belong to the same human capacity.

Use the guidebook's fields in this order:

- **Core meaning:** The situation or quality the card presents.
- **Deeper tension:** Where that quality becomes complicated.
- **Upright:** A constructive direction or active pattern.
- **Shadow / reversed:** A distortion, blockage, or alternative movement.
- **Reflection:** A question to help translate symbolism into lived experience.

### Numbers and court cards

**Ace:** A beginning, an opening, or a concentrated possibility.

**Two:** Relationship, comparison, a decision, or the work of holding two needs together.

**Three:** Expression, cooperation, or consequences beginning to spread.

**Four:** Stability, structure, rest, or a pattern becoming fixed.

**Five:** Disruption, conflict, loss, or a challenge to the existing arrangement.

**Six:** An adjustment through support, movement, memory, or recognition.

**Seven:** Evaluation, strategy, perseverance, or a test of discernment.

**Eight:** Sustained effort, movement, changing limits, or a decision with momentum.

**Nine:** Maturity, accumulated experience, fulfillment, or strain near a threshold.

**Ten:** A culmination that carries both results and a transition.

These associations are aids to comparison. The individual image determines how a number works in its suit.

**Page:** A learner, messenger, or fresh approach. Ask what needs curiosity and practice.

**Knight:** A quality in motion. Ask what the action accomplishes and what its speed overlooks.

**Queen:** A mature relationship with the suit's inner life. Ask how care, discernment, and boundaries work together.

**King:** Direction, stewardship, and responsibility for outward consequences. Ask how authority is earned and held accountable.

Court cards can describe an aspect of you, another person, or a way of responding. Their roles are available regardless of age or gender.

### Using the zodiac cards

For a traditional reading, shuffle only cards 00-77. Draw a zodiac card separately as a lens before or after the tarot spread.

For a combined reading, shuffle all 90 face cards. A zodiac card appearing in a position describes a temperament or tension relevant there. Read Aries as initiative and the risk of aggression, for example, whether or not anyone involved was born under Aries.

For a twelve-part reflection, lay out the zodiac cards in their usual sequence from Aries to Pisces. Draw one tarot card beside each to explore its question. Treat the arrangement as a symbolic exercise rather than a prediction of twelve fixed events.

### Spreads for this deck

#### The Choice at the Gate - three cards

1. **The call:** What worthwhile need or value is asking for attention?
2. **The temptation:** What appealing shortcut carries a hidden cost?
3. **The next right act:** What concrete response best serves the situation?

Compare the first and third cards. The next action may be smaller and less dramatic than the ideal, but it should move toward it. Read the temptation as a pattern to examine, not an accusation against a particular person.

#### The Sword and the Cup - four cards

1. **The truth:** What needs to be said or understood?
2. **The feeling:** What emotional reality needs acknowledgment?
3. **The boundary:** What limit makes honesty and care possible?
4. **The bridge:** How can you respond without surrendering either clarity or compassion?

This spread is useful before a difficult conversation. Write a sentence you can actually say, then check that it distinguishes facts from assumptions.

#### The Battlefield Within - five cards

1. **The pressure:** What is influencing your response?
2. **The light:** What constructive capacity can you draw on?
3. **The shadow:** Where that capacity could become distorted.
4. **The witness:** What evidence or perspective you need.
5. **The act:** A practical step you can take next.

Light and shadow may appear in the same suit. Courage and aggression, for example, can draw on the same energy. The witness position helps distinguish them through consequences.

#### The Reckoning and Repair - six cards

1. **What happened:** The pattern to recognize.
2. **My responsibility:** The part you can honestly own.
3. **What is not mine:** A limit to that responsibility.
4. **What repair needs:** A useful response to harm.
5. **What must change:** The condition that would otherwise repeat the pattern.
6. **What can grow:** A realistic direction after the reckoning.

Do not turn this spread into an argument for taking all the blame. The third position matters as much as the second. Repair may require an apology, restitution, a boundary, changed behavior, or accepting that reconciliation is not yours to demand.

#### The Sanctuary - five cards plus an optional zodiac lens

1. **What sustains you.**
2. **What drains you.**
3. **What boundary protects your reserves.**
4. **What support you can seek.**
5. **What you can offer sustainably.**

Draw a zodiac lens to examine the temperament influencing your care. Cancer may highlight protective attachment; Virgo may highlight the demand to do everything perfectly.

### Worked example

Question: "How can I respond to a conflict with a colleague without abandoning my principles?"

Suppose the Choice at the Gate yields **Justice**, **Five of Swords**, and **Queen of Cups**.

Justice in the call position asks for a fair account of what happened: dates, agreed responsibilities, and each person's perspective. Five of Swords in the temptation position warns against winning through humiliation or collecting supporters for a personal campaign. Queen of Cups as the next act suggests a calm conversation that acknowledges feeling while keeping a clear boundary.

A practical response might be: prepare the specific facts, ask for a private discussion, describe the impact without assigning motives, and propose an explicit agreement for next time. The reading has not established who is right. It has suggested a way to investigate and respond more fairly.

### Reading several cards together

Notice repeated suits. Many Wands can emphasize urgency and competing aims. Many Cups can emphasize emotional bonds. Many Swords can emphasize decisions or conflict in language. Many Pentacles can emphasize capacity, work, or material consequences.

Notice movement. The Eight of Cups walking away and the Six of Swords crossing water may together suggest a considered transition. Ask what each image carries forward and what it leaves.

Notice differences in power. Who offers, who receives, who can refuse, and who sets the terms? A generous-looking image can still ask about unequal control.

Notice the scale of the response. A Major Arcana may describe a large turning point while a Minor Arcana gives the practical step. The Tower with Eight of Pentacles can suggest rebuilding through patient work after a structure fails.

Finally, test the interpretation against reality. A reading should help you notice and act; it should not require facts to be rearranged to fit the story.

### Keeping a reading journal

Record the date, question, spread, cards, first impressions, and the action you chose. Return later to note what actually happened and what you learned. Include where the interpretation missed the situation.

Useful closing prompts:

- What did the reading help me notice?
- What claim still needs evidence?
- What action is within my control?
- Who else is affected by that action?
- What would make me revise my interpretation?

### The shared back

The back intertwines angelic and demonic wings around an eclipse and crossed swords. It holds the deck's central tension without deciding the draw in advance. It is not a face card and has no upright or reversed reading. Its paired composition is designed to feel balanced when the deck is turned; production checks document its actual symmetry.

## The card meanings

### 00 - The Fool
*Major Arcana | The First Choice*

**Core meaning:** A soul enters the war before understanding its cost. The angel offers guidance, the demon offers certainty, and the pilgrim must choose without surrendering judgment.

**Deeper tension:** Trust becomes wisdom when it notices consequences; fear can disguise itself as prudence.

**Upright:** Begin with a modest, honest step. Seek counsel, accept uncertainty, and keep responsibility for the choice.

**Shadow / reversed:** Recklessness or paralysis may be letting another voice choose for you. Check the real stakes before leaping or withdrawing.

**Reflection:** Which first step honors both your hope and your responsibility?

**Deeper tension:** Skill does not establish innocence. Good intentions need transparent methods.

**Upright:** Use what you know to make one helpful change. State your purpose clearly and allow others to question your methods.

**Shadow / reversed:** Charm, scattered effort, or hidden motives may be taking the place of honest work. Match your promises to evidence.

**Reflection:** Who benefits from the way you are using your influence?

*Major Arcana | The Quiet Witness*

**Upright:** Listen carefully, observe patterns, and allow unfinished knowledge time to develop. Protect privacy without abandoning accountability.

**Shadow / reversed:** Silence may conceal avoidance, or suspicion may be presenting itself as intuition. Look for evidence and speak when harm requires action.

**Reflection:** What do you know, what do you suspect, and what remains unknown?

## A closing practice

After the final card, put the deck down and name one action you can review later. The Eternal War returns to the same question in many forms: what does this choice make possible, and what does it ask of the people affected? Let your answer remain open to evidence, correction, and a better next act.
